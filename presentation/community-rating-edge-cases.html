💡 Proposed Solution: 5-Review Threshold

  • Hide ratings until 5+ reviews: Prevents misleading averages from 1-2 outlier reviews
  • Statistical validity: Minimum sample size needed for meaningful average that users can trust
  • Industry standard: Amazon, Yelp, and major platforms use 3-10 review thresholds before displaying ratings
  • Trust signal: Users inherently distrust "5.0★ (2 reviews)" but trust "4.5★ (234 reviews)"
  • Reduces gaming risk: Harder to manipulate ratings when threshold requires multiple reviews

❓ Strategic Questions

5 vs 10 threshold: Is 5 reviews enough for HelloFresh context, or should we require 10+ for higher confidence?
Low rating intervention: If a recipe hits 5+ reviews with poor rating (2-3★), do we investigate recipe quality or menu placement?

⚠️ Premium Recipe Upselling Risk

Business Risk: Premium recipes cost €3-5 more than standard meals. If ratings are visible and a premium recipe has a low rating, it creates direct conversion friction at the point of upsell decision.

⚠️ Business Impact Analysis

  • Direct revenue risk: Premium recipes generate €3-5 additional margin per portion — low ratings create immediate conversion friction
  • Comparison shopping behavior: Users compare premium vs standard recipes side-by-side. A 3.1★ premium vs 4.5★ standard makes the upsell decision obvious (choose standard)
  • Price-quality expectations: Premium pricing sets higher quality expectations — a mediocre rating (3.0-3.5★) feels worse on premium than standard recipes
  • Sample size matters MORE: Low-rated premium with 456 reviews is harder to dismiss than standard recipe with same rating — users assume "if THIS many people rated it poorly, it must really be bad"
  • Threshold strategy implications: Hiding ratings until 5+ reviews helps bootstrap, but once visible, poor premium ratings directly impact the highest-margin products

❓ Premium Strategy Questions

Different thresholds for premium? Should premium recipes require 10+ reviews before showing ratings (vs 5 for standard) to reduce early-rating volatility risk?
Quality intervention trigger: If a premium recipe hits 3.5★ or below with 10+ reviews, do we immediately investigate recipe quality or temporarily remove from menu?
A/B test impact: What's the conversion rate difference for premium recipes with visible vs hidden ratings? Worth measuring before full rollout.

2. Zero Rating Count - Bootstrapping Problem

Challenge: New recipes (0-4 reviews) need to collect ratings to reach the 5-review threshold, but without visible ratings, users may not be motivated to rate them.

💡 Bootstrap Strategies

  • Empty state with personality: Use friendly illustration to make the empty state feel inviting rather than barren
  • "No reviews yet" framing: Emphasize being first as an opportunity, not a problem — social proof through scarcity
  • Clear value proposition: Explain how their rating directly improves their meal recommendations
  • Large interactive stars: Make rating action prominent and easy to tap — 48px touch targets for mobile
  • Centered layout: Focus user attention on the call-to-action by removing visual distractions

3. Customized Recipe Ratings

Challenge: Users can customize recipes (swap proteins, remove ingredients, adjust portions), then rate the modified version. These ratings may not reflect the original recipe quality, creating misleading signals for other users.

💡 Proposed Solution

  • Customization indicator: Show clear badge at top of review when user customized the recipe
  • Specify what changed: Be explicit about customizations (e.g., "swapped from chicken" not just "customised")
  • Include in overall rating: Count customized ratings but tag them visibly for transparency
  • Separate filtering: Allow users to filter by "Original recipe only" or "All versions" when viewing reviews
  • Track at rating time: Ask "Did you customize this recipe?" before submission to capture data accurately

❓ Open Questions

Display threshold: Show customization badge on ALL customized reviews, or only "material changes" (protein swap, major ingredient removal)?
Rating validity: Should customized ratings count toward overall average, or be excluded entirely? Pro: more data. Con: less accuracy.
Positive signal: Could customization data actually help? (e.g., "Works great with beef too!" might encourage experimentation)
