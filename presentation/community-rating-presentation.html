Community Rating

Displaying community ratings during meal selection will increase decision confidence and create a virtuous cycle that motivates users to contribute more ratings, building a self-reinforcing feedback loop.

Part 1

Confidence in Meal Selection

Users have problems choosing meals confidently, especially with broader menus.

Problem
Customers lack confidence because they don't trust the system to select for them, and they feel overwhelmed when trying to do it themselves at scale. The expanded menu amplifies both sides of this tension.
Solution
Display community ratings as social proof to reduce decision friction and help users make confident choices faster, without relying solely on system preselection.
Hypothesis
Displaying community ratings during meal selection will increase decision confidence, especially as menus expand and choice complexity grows.

Part 2

A Bet for Rating Engagement

Users stop rating because they don't see the value. Displaying community ratings closes the loop and creates a virtuous cycle.

Problem
~80% of customers never rate a single recipe. The dominant pattern is "early then stop" — users rate initially, then stop when they don't see value of rating.
Hypothesis
Displaying community ratings demonstrates the direct value of providing feedback, creating a self-feeding flywheel that motivates users to contribute more ratings.
Solution
Make community ratings visible throughout meal selection so users see their contributions helping others make confident decisions, proving the collective value of rating.

Measurement

Metrics to Track

How we'll measure success for both meal selection confidence and rating engagement.

Meal Selection Confidence

  • • Box 1 to Box 2 conversion rate
  • • Settled Conversion rate
  • • Time to complete meal selection
  • • Decision confidence (qualitative)

Rating Engagement

  • • % of customers who rate at least once
  • • Recipe response rate
  • • Ratings per customer per week
  • • Reduction in "early then stop" pattern
