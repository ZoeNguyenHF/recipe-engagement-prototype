The Challenge

While the core experience is straightforward (show community ratings during meal selection), three edge case scenarios require strategic decisions that balance:

  • Revenue optimization - Converting browsers to purchasers
  • Personalization integrity - Maintaining trust in our recommendation engine
  • Rating quality - Ensuring credible, useful social proof

Decision 1: Low Volume - New Recipes

Scenario

A NEW recipe launches with 0-5 ratings. It appears in meal selection. Do we display the low/zero rating, hide the rating section entirely, or take another approach?

Why This Matters

Revenue concern: Hiding ratings loses conversion opportunity (+270% lift). Quality concern: Showing unreliable ratings (2 reviews) creates poor experience. Engagement concern: We need early adopters to rate these recipes to build credibility.

Solution 1: Display with "New - Be the first to rate" label

Show rating section with empty stars and clear signaling that this is a new recipe.

Pros Turns zero ratings into engagement opportunity • Transparent about recipe status • Drives flywheel from day 1
Cons No social proof yet • Requires elegant UI treatment
Solution 2: Hide rating until 15+ ratings collected

Don't show rating section at all until minimum threshold is reached.

Pros Protects quality bar • No confusing low-volume data • Conservative approach
Cons Delays conversion benefit • Slows rating collection • Inconsistent experience

Decision Needed

Do we prioritize engagement & transparency (Solution 1: display with label) or quality threshold (Solution 2: hide until 15+ ratings)?

Decision 2: Low Volume - Premium Recipes

Scenario

A PREMIUM recipe has 8 ratings (4.2★) after 6 weeks because smaller audience. It's a successful, high-quality recipe but lacks rating volume. Same question: display or hide?

Why This Matters

Premium recipes inherently have lower volume due to smaller market segment. If we hide ratings until high volume, premium may never show ratings. Yet premium customers may particularly value social proof from "people like me."

Note: If Decision 1 = Solution 1 (display with label for new recipes), same logic likely applies here. This becomes a fast confirmation rather than a separate debate.

Decision Needed

Should premium recipes follow the same logic as new recipes, or require different handling to maintain premium positioning?

Decision 3: Low-Rated Meals in Personalized Recommendations

Scenario

A meal with 2.1★ (1,200 ratings) appears in "Top Picks for You" because our algorithm knows YOU love it (you've rated similar recipes 5★). Do we show the low community rating in this personalized context?

Why This Matters

This is where personalization and transparency collide. Hiding the rating protects our recommendation engine but feels like we're hiding information. Showing the rating maintains transparency but might undermine customer trust in personalized picks.

Solution 1: Hide community rating in "Top Picks" zone

Don't display community ratings for meals in personalized recommendation sections.

Pros Protects personalization confidence • No customer confusion • Algorithm maintains authority
Cons Feels like hiding information • Inconsistent experience • Customers notice missing data
Solution 2: Show with personal context

Display: "2.1★ (1.2k) Community | You rated similar recipes 5★"

Pros Full transparency • Explains the recommendation • Educates customers about personalization
Cons May still undermine recommendation • Requires customer understanding of logic
Solution 3: Visual differentiation without stars

Green highlighted box + "Your top rated" badge. No community star rating displayed in this zone.

Pros Clear "this is personalized" signal • Doesn't hide data (visible elsewhere) • Maintains recommendation confidence
Cons Inconsistent star display • Customers may wonder why stars are missing

Decision Needed

This is a values decision about algorithm confidence vs. customer transparency. Which matters more in personalized recommendation contexts?

Decision 4: Personal vs Community Rating Conflict

Scenario

In "Your Ratings" history view, a recipe shows: You rated 5★, Community rated 2.1★ (1.2k). How do we display this on the card?

Why This Matters

This is the simplest decision - it's the customer's personal history, so their rating should dominate. But we need to confirm: do we show community rating at all in this context?

Solution 1: Personal rating primary, community secondary

Display: "5★ Your rating | 2.1★ (1.2k) Community" - Personal rating larger and first.

Pros Respects personal perspective • Provides context • Transparent about difference
Cons Slightly more complex UI • May prompt "why is mine different?" questions

Decision Needed

Confirm that personal rating takes priority in personal history contexts. (Low controversy - likely quick alignment.)
