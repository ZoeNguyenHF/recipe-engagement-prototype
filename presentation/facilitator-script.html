Pre-Meeting Setup & Preparation Before Session

📋 Pre-Meeting Checklist (24 hours before)

  • Send decision document to all attendees with subject: "3 Decisions Needed: Community Rating Edge Cases"
  • Pre-wire key stakeholders (15-min calls):
    • Personalization Lead Address "will ratings override our algorithm?" concern
    • Revenue Lead Get them excited about +270% lift data
    • Quality Lead Discuss proposed thresholds for rating display
  • Test prototype in browser - ensure all toggles work
  • Have edge-cases.html open in separate browser tab
  • Print one-page decision summary for yourself
  • Prepare "parking lot" doc for off-topic items

💡 Room Setup

Position yourself where you can easily see all faces. Have a whiteboard or digital doc visible for capturing decisions in real-time. This isn't a "watch the presenter" meeting - it's a working session.

🎯 Your Goal

Leave with 3-4 documented decisions, no blockers to M1 development, and clear DRI for any follow-ups. You are NOT asking for permission - you're gathering strategic input on execution details.

Part 1 The Case: Business Impact & The Ask 8 minutes

🖥️ What to Display

SLIDE community-rating-presentation.html - scroll to metrics sections

📢 Opening (2 min)

"Good morning. Thanks for making time. Before we dive into how we're building this, let me frame why it matters and what we need from this session."

PAUSE - SCROLL TO METRICS

"The business case for community ratings is validated. Point to metrics 270% conversion lift from displaying ratings - that's industry benchmark from Spiegel Research. 19 to 22 percentage point lift on Box 2 retention - that's our strongest causal retention lever. And we saw 43% higher add-to-cart rate when we tested ratings in the top recipe widget."

"The prototype is built. Gesture forward You'll see it in a moment - it works, it's ready to test."

"Leadership has greenlit this initiative. We're moving forward."

⚡ CRITICAL FRAMING MOMENT

"What we need from you today: strategic decisions on three edge case scenarios that define HOW we display ratings. These decisions unblock M1 development. That's why we're here - to make those decisions together."

📢 Problem + Solution (3 min)

"Quick context on the problem we're solving - you've seen this in the pre-read, so I'll be brief."

"Two core problems: Hold up one finger Customers lack confidence in meal selection, especially as menus expand. They don't trust the system to preselect for them, but they feel overwhelmed selecting themselves. Hold up second finger About 80% of customers never rate a single recipe. The pattern is 'early then stop' - they rate initially, then stop when they don't see the value."

"One solution addresses both: Lean forward slightly Display community ratings during meal selection. This gives customers social proof to make confident choices, AND it closes the feedback loop - they see their ratings helping others, which motivates more rating."

"It's a self-reinforcing flywheel. Decision confidence drives rating behavior, rating behavior creates more social proof, more social proof drives more confidence. That's the hypothesis."

🎯 Check the Room

Scan for nodding heads or confused looks. If someone looks puzzled, pause and ask: "Any questions on the problem framing before we see the prototype?"

📢 The Ask (3 min)

"Here's specifically what we need to decide today. Count on fingers Three edge case scenarios:"

"One: How do we handle new recipes that have zero or very low rating volume? Do we display the rating section, hide it, or take another approach?"

"Two: What happens when a low-rated meal appears in personalized recommendations? Do we show the community rating that might undermine the recommendation, or handle it differently?"

"Three: How do we display ratings when there's a big gap between someone's personal rating and the community rating?"

PAUSE 3 SECONDS

"These aren't hypotheticals - these situations will happen daily once we launch. The decisions we make define the customer experience and affect different business outcomes. Make eye contact with key stakeholders Revenue, personalization, quality - all three are in tension here. That's why we need strategic input from this group."

"Everything you'll see today is decision-ready. We've researched the options, built the prototypes, mapped the trade-offs. Lean back, open posture Your role is to provide strategic direction on which trade-offs we should prioritize."

🛡️ If Someone Says: "Let's just test everything"

Response: "Absolutely open to testing - that's built into the M1 plan. But we need to build a baseline first. We can't test all permutations simultaneously. Which solution should be our default hypothesis that we build and test first?"

🛡️ If Someone Says: "Don't we need more data first?"

Response: "What specific data would change your perspective on [the edge case]? PAUSE We have industry benchmarks, our own widget test results, and 12 out of 12 customers in research asking for this. What additional data would help you feel comfortable making a directional decision today?"

Gesture broadly Everything appearing on this screen is already filtered by our algorithm based on taste profile, dietary needs, past behavior.   • If someone raises concern: Listen fully, acknowledge, then: "Good point - let's see if the edge case solutions address that."
  • If someone tries to derail into details: "Excellent question - let me capture that for implementation discussion. Write it on parking lot For today, I need strategic direction on the three edge cases. Let's dive in."

🛡️ If Personalization Stakeholder Pushes Back

Response: "I hear the concern about ratings potentially overriding your algorithm. Let me be direct: we're not replacing personalization logic - we're making it more transparent. Right now, customers don't understand WHY something is recommended. Ratings give context. And the data backs this up - those 12 customers in research specifically said they want ratings reflected in recommendations. They're asking us to show this."

Part 3 Edge Case Decisions 18-20 minutes

🖥️ What to Display

SLIDE edge-cases.html - All sections collapsed, expand one at a time

📢 Framework Setup (1 min)

"Alright. Decision time. Open edge-cases.html I'm going to walk through each edge case systematically. For each one, I'll show you the scenario, the options we've considered with pros and cons, and our recommended approach - marked 'PROPOSED.' Then we'll make a decision before moving to the next one."

"I need clear direction from this group on which option to build. If you disagree with the proposed approach, tell me what you'd prefer and why. Lean forward Let's keep debate focused and make decisions."

💡 Pacing Strategy

Edge Cases 1A and 1B should go fast (3-4 min each). Edge Case 2 is where you'll spend time (6-7 min) because it's the highest tension. Edge Case 3 is a quick confirmation (3 min).

Edge Case 1A: Low Volume - New Recipes (5-6 min)

🖥️ Action

Click to expand "Edge Case 1A" section. Let them see the visual issue with new recipes showing low rating counts.

📢 Frame the Scenario (30 sec)

"First edge case: Gesture to screen We launch a NEW recipe. Zero ratings on day one. Maybe 2 ratings by day two. It appears in meal selection. Question: Do we show the rating section with zero or low counts, hide it entirely until we hit a threshold, or do something else?"

📢 Surface the Tension (30 sec)

"Here's the tension: Revenue perspective - hiding ratings loses that 270% conversion lift. We're leaving money on the table. Quality perspective - showing 2 ratings feels unreliable. Poor customer experience. Both are valid concerns."

📢 Walk Through Solutions (2 min)

Scroll to Solution 1

"Our proposed approach - Solution 1: Display the rating section but with a 'New - Be the first to rate' label. Point to visual Empty stars, clear signaling that this is new."

"Pros: Turns zero ratings into an engagement opportunity. Transparent about status. Drives the flywheel from day one because we're explicitly asking early adopters to rate. Cons: There's no social proof yet, obviously. And it requires elegant UI treatment."

PAUSE - SCROLL TO SOLUTION 2

"Alternative - Solution 2: Hide the rating section entirely until we collect 15 or more ratings. Gesture to threshold Conservative approach."

"Pros: Protects quality bar. No confusing low-volume data. Cons: We delay the conversion benefit for potentially weeks. We slow rating collection because no one knows to rate these recipes. And we create an inconsistent experience - some recipes show ratings, some don't."

⚡ DECISION FORCING (3 min)

"Show of hands: Who supports Solution 1 - display with 'New' label? PAUSE - COUNT HANDS"

"Who prefers Solution 2 - hide until 15 ratings? PAUSE - COUNT HANDS"

"Anyone strongly opposed to Solution 1? PAUSE 5 SECONDS"

🎯 If There's Debate

  • Let it run for 60-90 seconds max
  • Identify who's speaking - which stakeholder concern are they representing?
  • Ask directly: "Name, what would you need to see to feel comfortable with Solution 1?"
  • If stuck, offer: "Can we test both in M1 with a 50/50 split?" (Have this ready as backup)

📢 Document Decision

"Okay, I'm hearing [majority preference]. Name, can you confirm that's aligned with your perspective? WAIT FOR CONFIRMATION Great. Documented: Solution [X] for new recipes. Type it visibly or write on whiteboard Moving on."

🛡️ If Quality Lead Resists Showing Low Ratings

Response: "I understand protecting quality experience. What if we add a minimum threshold - say, don't show any numerical rating until 5 ratings, but DO show 'New - Be first to rate' from day one? That way we're not displaying potentially unreliable 1-rating scores, but we're still driving engagement. Would that address your concern?"

🖥️ Action

Click to expand "Edge Case 1B" section.

📢 Frame the Scenario (30 sec)

"Related scenario: PREMIUM recipes. Gesture to screen 8 ratings, 4.2 stars, been live for 6 weeks. It's a successful, high-quality recipe. But low volume because smaller audience - premium has inherent volume constraints. Same question: display or hide?"

📢 Address Premium Positioning Concern

"I hear the concern about premium positioning. Let me reframe: Not showing ratings doesn't make recipes MORE premium - it just hides social proof from premium customers who may particularly value 'people like me' validation. Point to Solution 1 visual This maintains premium feel with the badge while showing transparency. That's consistent with premium brand values - quality and honesty."

Edge Case 2: Low-Rated Meals in Recommendations (6-7 min)

💡 This is the High-Tension Decision

Expect debate. This is where personalization and transparency collide. Go slower. Let people talk. Your job is to facilitate toward a decision, not to win.

🖥️ Action

Click to expand "Edge Case 2" section. Let visual load fully before speaking.

📢 Frame the Scenario (1 min)

"Harder scenario. Point to screen A meal with 2.1 stars - 1,200 ratings, so this is statistically reliable data - appears in 'Top Picks for You' because our algorithm knows YOU love it. Your taste profile, your history, your dietary needs - the algorithm is confident this is right for you even though the community rated it low."

"Do we show the 2.1-star rating in that context? Or handle it differently?"

PAUSE 3 SECONDS

"Make eye contact around room This is where personalization and transparency collide. Let's talk through it."

📢 Walk Through Solutions (2.5 min)

Scroll to Solution 1

"Solution 1: Hide community rating in 'Top Picks' zones. Don't show the stars at all when something appears in a personalized recommendation section."

"Pros: Protects personalization confidence. No customer confusion. Algorithm maintains authority. Cons: Pause for effect It feels like we're hiding information. Customers notice missing data - they're not stupid. And it creates inconsistent experience."

Scroll to Solution 2

"Solution 2: Show with personal context. Display '2.1 stars community, but you rated similar recipes 5 stars.' Point to visual Full transparency, explains WHY we're recommending despite low rating."

"Pros: Transparent. Educates customers about how personalization works. Cons: May still undermine confidence in the recommendation. Some customers might ignore their own history and reject based on community rating."

PAUSE - SCROLL TO SOLUTION 3

"Solution 3 - our proposal: Visual differentiation. Point to green box and badge Green highlighted box, 'Your top rated' badge. No community star rating displayed in this specific zone."

"Pros: Clear 'this is personalized FOR YOU' signal. Doesn't hide data - the rating is visible elsewhere on the page if they click in. Maintains recommendation confidence. Cons: Inconsistent star display. Customers may wonder why stars are missing from Top Picks."

⚡ DECISION FORCING (3 min)

"This is a values decision about what we prioritize: algorithm confidence versus customer transparency. PAUSE 3 SECONDS"

"Personalization Lead, what's your comfort level with Solution 3 - the green highlighting without stars?"

WAIT FOR RESPONSE - DO NOT FILL SILENCE

🎯 Manage the Discussion

  • If Personalization Lead supports Solution 3: Lock it in quickly and move on
  • If they prefer Solution 1 (hide rating): Ask Revenue Lead their perspective
  • If they prefer Solution 2 (show with context): Ask if they're concerned it undermines recommendations
  • Let debate run for 2-3 minutes, then force decision

📢 Close the Decision

"I'm hearing [summarize positions]. Here's what I propose: We proceed with Solution 3 for M1 - green highlighting, no stars in Top Picks zone. We monitor feedback closely in the first two weeks. If we see customers confused or recommendations suffering, we have Solution 1 as a rollback option built into the feature flag. Can we align on that approach? Look directly at Personalization Lead Does that work for you?"

WAIT FOR AGREEMENT

"Confirmed: Solution 3 with monitoring. Document it Next edge case."

🛡️ If Personalization Lead Says "This Undermines Our Work"

Response: "I hear that concern. Let me push back gently: customers ALREADY don't understand your recommendations. We hear 'why is this in my box?' all the time. Solution 3 doesn't undermine personalization - it contextualizes it. The green box and 'Your top rated' badge say 'trust us, this is for YOU even if the crowd disagrees.' That's actually STRONGER messaging for personalization than what we have today. And we're not showing the conflicting rating right there - we're visually differentiating. Thoughts?"

🛡️ If Revenue Lead Pushes for Solution 2 (Show Everything)

Response: "I appreciate the transparency instinct. My concern with Solution 2 is we undercut our own recommendations, which are a core retention driver. That 19-22pp Box 2 retention effect comes from good meal selection - if customers start ignoring Top Picks because they see low community ratings, we hurt retention more than we gain in transparency. Solution 3 gives visual signal without undercutting. Make sense?"

🛡️ "How does this affect Menu team's recipe selection?"

🛡️ "What's the rollout risk? What if this backfires?"

Response: "M1 is controlled rollout to a percentage of customers - we're not flipping this on for everyone at once. Built-in A/B testing to validate business impact. And we have feature flags - if we see issues, we can turn it off within hours. Low technical risk, high learning opportunity."
